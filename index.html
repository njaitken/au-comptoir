Au Comptoir

Short, everyday café conversations. The server says hello, asks how you are, takes your order and says goodbye. You answer out loud in French. Each line gets three tries before it stops and shows you what it was listening for.

Choose a scene

Every scene follows the same basic shape

  1. Hello: Bonjour ! → Bonjour !
  2. How are you: Comment allez-vous ? → Très bien, merci. Et vous ?
  3. What can I get you: Qu'est-ce que je vous sers ? → Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.
  4. One or two simple questions: for here or to go, anything else, how you're paying
  5. Goodbye: Merci, bonne journée ! → Merci, au revoir !

Good to know

  • Bonjour comes first, every time, before you ask for anything. After about 6 p.m. it becomes bonsoir.
  • Vous with staff, tu with friends. The scene with a friend shows the switch.
  • Je voudrais… (I'd like) is the safe, polite way to order. Je vais prendre… and just un café, s'il vous plaît are also normal, and all are accepted.
  • Un café is a small espresso. Un café crème is coffee with steamed milk.
  • The bill won't come on its own. Ask: l'addition, s'il vous plaît.
  • In a busy Paris café the server may skip "how are you" and go straight to your order. The scenes keep it in so you practise it.
  • Films and TV are great for hearing real speed and slang, but their café scenes are usually too fast and too jokey for a first conversation. These scenes follow beginner course dialogues instead.

Practise live with Claude

For an open conversation, copy this, start Claude's voice mode, and paste or read it out.

Let's role-play a simple café visit in French for a beginner. You are the server and use "vous". Keep every line short and basic. Go in this order: say hello, ask how I am, ask what you can get me, ask one simple follow-up (for here or to go, or anything else), give me the price, and say goodbye. After each of my lines: if what I said isn't close to correct French for that moment, say "Pardon ?" and ask again. After three tries on the same line, tell me in English what you were expecting and how to pronounce it, then move on. At the end, list the words I struggled with.

How grading works: a French speech recognizer turns your voice into text, which is compared word by word with the expected line and its accepted variants. A low score means a French listener probably wouldn't catch it. It measures how understandable you are, not a lab-grade phonetic score.
